# Source, scope and method — Visual Design

This pack synthesizes the seven composition principles and seven visual elements in Digital Polo’s [Principles of Design](https://www.digitalpolo.com/principles-of-design-explained/) and a **curated subset of design-relevant posts** linked in `articles.json`. It does not claim to ingest every Digital Polo post or that these heuristics are a complete academic visual-design theory.

## What is independently authored here

The issue taxonomy, original remediation recipes, worked CSS/HTML examples, cross-principle conflict resolutions, test questions, machine-readable records and agent routing instructions are independent synthesis for **frontend web software**, not reproductions of the articles. Each article entry records its original URL and a narrow operational takeaway. No third-party articles, images, design assets or fonts are packaged. Source titles and links are attribution, not endorsement or transfer of copyright.

## Source versus engineering judgment

The original article uses **balance, contrast, emphasis, movement, pattern, proportion and unity**. The seven visual elements are **line, shape, form, color, value, texture and space**. Color harmony schemes and 60/30/10, modular scale choices, one/two-font guidance, Gestalt grouping and focal-point rules are **optional starting heuristics**. They are not hard requirements, provable psychology of every viewer, nor indicators of WCAG conformance.

Additional requirements are deliberately added from frontend engineering practice: CSS reflow, semantic tokens, keyboard/DOM order, localization, dark mode, asset licensing, and interactive component states. For WCAG compliance, activate the separate `wcag21-*` skills; there is no certification in this pack. For task usability, use `ux-*` skills. Behavioral tests and actual users take precedence over visual speculation.

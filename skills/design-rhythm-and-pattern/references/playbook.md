# Pattern, repetition and rhythm playbook

## Diagnose
- Are similar controls/cards/icons styled as though they belong to one component family?
- Does repetitive background ornament compete with content?
- Do recurring elements create predictable rhythm but inadvertently hide real priority?

## Repair
1. Choose repeated invariants: typography roles, spacing scale, component radius, icon stroke, image treatment. Reuse existing tokens/components rather than new one-offs.
2. Distinguish **repetition** (a role consistently rendered) from **pattern** (that repetition arranged into a motif or deliberate cadence). Pattern is optional; consistency of UI semantics is not.
3. Introduce variety only along meaningful dimensions: featured content, state and level of information. If every item is identical, improve content differentiation instead of decorative randomness.
4. For ornamental repeat backgrounds, constrain opacity, size and contrast; do not place noisy texture beneath important text or animate patterns unnecessarily.
5. Compare three screens side by side for same-role consistency and intentional differences.

## Acceptance
Users can identify same-role elements across surfaces, important variations remain visible, decorative repetition does not obscure text or reduce usability, and cross-page screenshots retain unity.
